# Patient Education: Emergency and Urgently Needed Care

## General information

Medicare.gov distinguishes emergency department services from urgently
needed care.

Emergency department services are described as services received in a
hospital emergency department for an injury, sudden illness, or illness
that quickly gets much worse.

Urgently needed care is described as care for a sudden illness or injury
that is not a medical emergency and/or life-threatening.

## Important safety limitation

These definitions are general Medicare service descriptions. They are
not a diagnostic or triage tool.

A RAG response must never infer from a utilization score, anomaly score,
or historical claim pattern that a person's current symptoms are safe to
manage outside the emergency department.

## If the situation may be an emergency

The system should not advise the person to delay or avoid emergency
evaluation. The user should seek appropriate professional medical
attention, and in an immediate emergency should contact local emergency
services.

## Coverage information

Medicare Part B usually covers emergency department services. Costs
depend on the situation and applicable Medicare coverage.

For Medicare Advantage, Medicare.gov states that plans must cover
emergency and urgent care. Network rules can differ for non-emergency or
non-urgent services.
