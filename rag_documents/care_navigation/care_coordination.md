# Care Coordination and Navigation

## Purpose

This document provides general, source-backed information for
care-navigation responses. It is not a clinical decision rule and must
not be used to determine whether an emergency department (ED) visit was
medically necessary, avoidable, or inappropriate.

## Care coordination

The Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (AHRQ) describes care
coordination as deliberately organizing patient-care activities and
sharing information among participants involved in a person's care so
that needs and preferences are communicated to the right people at the
right time.

For this project, care-navigation responses may therefore focus on: -
helping a member understand which type of healthcare service may be
relevant; - encouraging coordination with the member's established
healthcare team; - supporting follow-up after healthcare encounters; -
identifying questions the member can ask a clinician, care manager, or
insurer; - helping the member understand available Medicare information.

The system should not diagnose, triage a specific patient, or tell a
person to avoid emergency care.

## Care-transition concepts

CMS describes advanced primary care management as including care
management, care coordination, patient-centered care planning, and
coordination of transitions between healthcare settings. CMS
specifically includes follow-up after an emergency-department visit and
follow-up after discharge from healthcare facilities among
care-transition activities.

For a navigation workflow, appropriate non-clinical actions can
include: 1. Confirm that the member has a primary care or
care-management contact. 2. Encourage follow-up according to the
discharge or clinician instructions. 3. Encourage the member to keep
medication and appointment information available. 4. Help identify
questions or barriers to discuss with the care team. 5. Escalate urgent
or emergency concerns to appropriate healthcare services rather than
attempting to resolve them through the navigator.

## Source boundaries

The RAG system should prefer authoritative sources and return the source
title/link with retrieved answers. It should not invent provider names,
appointment availability, benefits, costs, or clinical recommendations.
