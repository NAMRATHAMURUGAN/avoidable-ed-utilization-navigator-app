# Medicare Service Navigation

## Purpose

This document summarizes general Medicare service-navigation information
relevant to the project. It is informational and does not determine
clinical necessity.

## Emergency and urgent care

Medicare.gov states that Part B usually covers emergency department
services. Emergency department services are described as services
received in a hospital emergency department for an injury, sudden
illness, or illness that quickly gets much worse.

Medicare.gov also describes urgently needed care as care for a sudden
illness or injury that is not a medical emergency and/or
life-threatening.

The navigator should not classify a member's specific symptoms as an
emergency or non-emergency. Instead, it should direct the member to
appropriate professional care and provide source-backed general
information.

## Medicare Advantage

Medicare.gov explains that Medicare Advantage plans must cover emergency
and urgent care and almost all medically necessary services covered by
Original Medicare. Network rules can differ for non-emergency or
non-urgent care.

When answering coverage questions, the RAG system should distinguish: -
Original Medicare; - Medicare Advantage; - emergency care; - urgent
care; - routine/non-urgent care.

It should avoid stating a member's exact out-of-pocket cost unless that
information is available from an authoritative, plan-specific source.

## Getting Medicare services

Medicare.gov recommends checking whether a provider accepts Medicare
and, for Medicare Advantage, checking the plan's network rules.
Emergency and urgent care can be obtained when needed even if a provider
or hospital is not in-network.

## Safe response pattern

When a user asks where to obtain care: 1. Provide general
service-category information from the retrieved source. 2. State that
the appropriate choice depends on the person's situation. 3. For
possible emergencies, do not discourage ED/emergency services. 4.
Encourage consultation with a qualified healthcare professional. 5.
Include the authoritative source used.
